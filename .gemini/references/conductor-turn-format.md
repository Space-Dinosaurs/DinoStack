# Conductor Turn Format

## Purpose

The conductor's default output is noise. Every operator turn read is attention spent; a status-only turn spends that attention for no return, and an unstructured turn forces the operator to rebuild context on every check-in - a cost multiplied across many concurrent sessions. This file exists to guard that attention. North Star pillar 1 states the requirement directly (`docs/overview/vision.md:19`):

> "Guard operator attention. Surface decisions and work-stoppages, not status."

Two mechanisms enforce this: a warrant-bound shape for any turn that IS written (Rule A, §4), and an emission gate that decides whether a turn should be written at all (Rule B, §2). Neither rule replaces judgment - both are the mechanical form of the same North Star sentence.

**DS-156 note.** An earlier version of Rule A added a structured block to every turn without removing the surrounding prose - structure was added, nothing was subtracted, and the net effect was a *bigger* turn to read, the exact regression pillar 1 forbids. §4 below fixes this by binding shape to which warrant fired: an execution turn (Decision/Stoppage/Completion, no Answer) is the structured block and nothing else; an Answer turn is prose and nothing else. The two shapes never mix except at the very end, where `## Operator decisions` may still close either one.

## The four warrants (Rule B)

A conductor turn is warranted only when at least one of these four conditions holds. Each has a one-line test:

1. **Decision** - a `## Operator decisions` heading is present in the turn.
2. **Stoppage** - a `Waiting:` line is present in the turn (see §7, forced yield).
3. **Completion** - the turn contains `[phase: complete]`, an explicit terminal-completion phrase anywhere in the turn (including the Conductor template's own `State: complete` field), OR (DS-156) the turn's identity line ITSELF opens with a short terminal declaration - "Done.", "Verification complete.", "Both PRs are done and verified.", "Merged and fully cleaned up.". **A conductor turn never carries `Status: DONE`** - that string is the engineer-role return contract's terminal enum value (`content/agents/engineer.md`), and its only possible appearance in a conductor turn is a RELAY of a subagent's return text ("The engineer returned Status: DONE, spawning the Skeptic now.") - a still-in-progress turn the guard must keep flagging, not a warrant of its own; `hooks/enforce-turn-shape.py` deliberately does not treat that string as a completion signal. A bare `done`, `shipped`, or `merged` occurring MID-MESSAGE (not on the identity line) still does **not** count - those words commonly appear in ordinary status prose ("unit 2 merged", "PR merged, pulling main") and are too weak a signal on their own to carry the completion warrant. A completion claim is further VETOED outright when a continuing-work signal is present anywhere in the turn - a non-empty `Running:` field, or a phrase such as "one to go", "remaining after", or "is/are still running" - closing the shape where the completion declaration is genuinely sentence-complete on the identity line but the body separately says the dependent work is still in flight ("Security audit complete." ... "Skeptic is still running."). See §10's hook-contract entry for the corpus measurement (main-agent population only - this hook never evaluates subagent/sidechain turns) behind both the identity-line restriction and the continuing-work veto.
4. **Answer** - the turn contains a quoted fragment responding to the operator's immediately-preceding message, OR (DS-155) the operator's most recent genuine message looks like a direct question AND is still the IMMEDIATELY preceding turn boundary - no genuinely separate, earlier completed assistant turn since it was asked. A stale question (several later background-agent check-in turns have happened since) grants nothing; see §10 for the corpus-measured fix to this recency check (it correctly ignores the model's own earlier narration-then-tool-call steps within the SAME turn, while still catching a genuinely stale-by-one question). This is the weakest of the four and best-effort only; it exists so a direct question never goes unanswered, not as a loophole for narration.

Everything else - agent spawned, agent returned, phase advanced, unit merged, CI green - is a silent continue. No turn is written for it.

## Scope filter

A conductor turn reports on **this session's work only**. Do not mention other concurrent sessions, other tickets, or unrelated in-flight work - the operator is tracking those elsewhere and a cross-session mention adds cost without adding a decision. Do not include rationale unless a decision surfaced in the *same* turn depends on it; rationale for past decisions belongs in the PR body, the plan artifact, or a memory file (see §5, bullet 3).

## Warrant-bound shape (Rule A)

Which of the four warrants fired (§2) determines the turn's shape, not just whether a turn happens at all. Bind on the Answer warrant first: **Answer always wins the shape question**, regardless of what else co-fires. A turn carrying the Answer warrant is an **Answer turn**, even if a Decision, Stoppage, or Completion warrant also fired on the same turn. A turn with no Answer warrant but at least one of Decision/Stoppage/Completion present is an **execution turn**. A turn with none of the four warrants present is neither shape: per Rule B (§2) it should not have been written at all, and §9's hook contract routes it to `_status_only_flag` rather than to either shape check - see §9's classification-order bullet for the full three-way gate.

### Execution turns

An execution turn (Answer warrant ABSENT, at least one of Decision/Stoppage/Completion PRESENT) follows this fixed order, and **the structured block IS the entire turn - zero prose**:

1. **Identity line first**: `ticket · branch · [phase: x]`. The `[phase: label]` breadcrumb itself is governed by `content/references/subagent-protocol.md` Rule 6 - read it there for firing points and the crash-recovery rationale; it is not restated here to avoid two copies drifting. **This is a documented CONVENTION, not a mechanically-enforced one (DS-155 round 3).** `hooks/enforce-turn-shape.py` no longer flags a missing or malformed identity line - see §10's history note for why the mechanical check was removed rather than repaired a third time. Following it remains expected practice; nothing on this repo's Stop-hook path will tell the conductor when it does not.
2. **Status slots**: `State`, `Running`, `Blocked` - one line each, omitted when empty. Capped at **1-3 status lines per turn**, with the forced-yield shape (§7) as the sole exception to the cap itself, not merely to its line contents: a forced-yield turn drops `State`/`Running`/`Blocked` entirely and instead carries one `Waiting:` line per agent, however many that is - the count is unbounded, not re-capped at 1-3.
3. **`## Operator decisions`, last.** Placement and internal formatting are governed by `content/sections/02-delegation.md` - read it there rather than here. When present, it terminates the turn: nothing follows it.

**The status region is every non-blank, unfenced line after the identity line and before `## Operator decisions` (or before end-of-message when no decisions heading is present)** - the block governed by step 2 above (`State`/`Running`/`Blocked`) plus any `Waiting:` lines. This is the same region `hooks/enforce-turn-shape.py` computes via `_segment`/`_regions`; a trailing pre-heading aside sits INSIDE it, not outside.

**"One line" understates how much content a single physical line can hold: markdown soft-wraps, so one line routinely renders as a full paragraph, not a short sentence.** Before DS-156 this file described the gap as "a full narrative sentence" at position 1, which itself understated the same margin - a single unbroken line can carry many sentences with no line break at all. `STATUS_LINE_MAX_CHARS` (below) is the fix for this specific margin: it bounds physical-line CONTENT LENGTH, not visual line count, so a soft-wrapped 200-character line is now caught the same way a hard-wrapped one would be.

**The identity line itself is exempt from SHAPE validation by construction, not by rule, but it is NOT exempt from the LENGTH bound below (DS-156, contract).** `_segment` takes the first non-blank line, whatever its content, to BE the identity line, and the status region starts strictly after it. Because the identity line never enters the status region, it was previously invisible to `_execution_prose_flag` in every respect - shape AND length. `STATUS_LINE_MAX_CHARS` (defined below, in exactly one place) closes the length half of that gap: a position-1 line exceeding it is flagged **on every execution-turn branch, not only the general branch** - the bound is a property of position 1 itself, not of which branch (§9) is running, so a sole-stoppage/forced-yield turn cannot evade it by using its wider raw-line domain to smuggle an over-length line into position 1. The line's SHAPE (whether it looks like `ticket · branch · [phase: x]`) is still never checked, on either branch. A length bound is not a shape predicate - it does not reopen DS-155's rejected identity-line shape check (`_session_has_established_identity` / `_session_has_active_ticket_context`, both deleted; see §10's history note), it only denies an unboundedly long line at position 1 the ability to evade every check downstream of it. See the Known uncovered shapes table below (row: single-line preamble) for what remains open after this bound (a short, well-formed-length line at position 1 can still say anything).

**`STATUS_LINE_MAX_CHARS = 200` (DS-156) is defined exactly once, here - every other reference to it in this file, including §9's hook-contract bullets, points back to this paragraph rather than re-asserting its own scope.** It bounds two distinct things: the identity line at position 1, on BOTH the sole-stoppage and the general branch of the `_execution_prose_flag` check (position 1 is checked the same way regardless of which branch owns the rest of the turn); and a `State:`/`Running:`/`Blocked:` slot line, which exists only in the general branch's status region (the sole-stoppage branch permits no slot lines at all - see below). A slot line, or the identity line, exceeding 200 characters is itself a shape violation under `_execution_prose_flag`, the same finding class as an unrecognized label. 200 is chosen to be generous for a genuine status line - well past what a single sentence needs - while still failing a multi-sentence narration: the DS-156 note in the Purpose section above (§1, ~94 words / ~570 characters of normative prose describing the earlier defect, not a quoted turn example) illustrates a narration comfortably past 500 characters. This is a NEW constraint on a NEW check (the general-branch structural shape check, which did not exist as a blocking check before DS-156), not a retroactive import of an existing bound onto unrelated behavior: `Waiting:` lines remain deliberately UNBOUNDED in length in the shape check (see the next paragraph) - round 2 of DS-156 (recorded in the paragraph below) established that importing a length bound onto `Waiting:` lines would convert what is today a silent pass into a block on the hook's only forced-yield path, a behavior change nobody has authorized. `STATUS_LINE_MAX_CHARS` is scoped exclusively to `State:`/`Running:`/`Blocked:` slot lines and the identity line; it does not touch `Waiting:` lines, fenced content, or `## Operator decisions` items, each of which is governed by its own, separately-justified rule elsewhere in this file.

**Every non-blank, unfenced line in the status region of an execution turn must be a `State:`/`Running:`/`Blocked:` slot line no longer than `STATUS_LINE_MAX_CHARS` (defined above), or a `Waiting:` line matching the shape check's own test - a `waiting` label, a colon, then non-whitespace content, with NO character-length test on `Waiting:` lines in the shape check itself.**

The sole-stoppage case is a DIFFERENT, WIDER domain, not a narrower whitelist over the same lines: when `stoppage` is the SOLE warrant present (no decision, completion, or answer also fired on the same turn), the check inspects every non-blank RAW line after the identity line, fenced or not - the same domain `_forced_yield_flag` inspects today via `_body_after_identity_line` - and only a `Waiting:` line matching the shape above is permitted there; a `State:`/`Running:`/`Blocked:` slot line, or any other content whether fenced or unfenced, is itself a shape violation. This wider, fence-blind domain is deliberate: it preserves §7's forced-yield requirement (DS-122's founding rule) exactly as `_forced_yield_flag` enforces it today, rather than narrowing to the fence-aware status region and silently opening a bypass for fenced content on a sole-stoppage turn. The identity line at position 1 sits BEFORE this raw-line domain, not inside it, and is covered by the shared `STATUS_LINE_MAX_CHARS` bound defined above regardless: a sole-stoppage turn's `Waiting:` lines stay length-unbounded exactly as before, but the identity line preceding them does not.

**The 120-character bound (`WAITING_LINE_MAX_CHARS`, named in §7 and in §9's free-pool table) is NOT part of either shape check above.** It governs free-of-charge status in the ADVISORY volume check only (§9). A `Waiting:` line longer than 120 characters still satisfies both shape checks and never blocks; it only loses its free-of-charge status under the volume check and charges like an ordinary line. This is a deliberate choice, not an oversight: converting a silent volume-check pass into a hard block would be a behavior change on a legitimately long agent descriptor, which nobody has authorized.

On the general (non-sole-stoppage) execution-turn whitelist, anything other than a recognized slot line within `STATUS_LINE_MAX_CHARS`, or a shape-matching `Waiting:` line - a narrative sentence, a caveat, a "meanwhile" aside, a restated rationale, or an over-length slot/identity line - is a shape violation, regardless of which execution warrant(s) fired. This is the structural predicate `_execution_prose_flag` mechanizes; see §9.

**The 1-3 line cap applies to the status slots only.** `## Operator decisions`, when present, is **additional** to that cap, not counted against it. This is a deliberate asymmetry, not an oversight: the kernel paragraph in `content/sections/02-delegation.md` that governs the Operator decisions block mandates a multi-line format per decision item - "the recommended action, one line of why, and the reversal offer" - and explicitly forbids imposing a numeric cap on the number of items, because a cap with no overflow rule would mechanically force the conductor to hide a decision. Reading the 1-3 line cap here as applying to the whole turn, decisions block included, would put this file in direct contradiction with that kernel paragraph. It does not: the cap governs `State`/`Running`/`Blocked` only.

This prose cap is narrower than what the mechanical hook (§10) actually bounds. `hooks/enforce-turn-shape.py`'s volume check (DS-151) computes a single whole-message CHARGE - every non-blank line, in either the status region or the `## Operator decisions` region, contributes 0 or 1 to the charge unless it participates in one of three named, individually-capped free pools (aggregate fenced content in the status region, well-formed `Waiting:` lines when stoppage is the sole warrant, and up to `ITEM_FREE_LINES` lines per recognized decision item) - and compares that single number against one flat budget, `BASE_BODY_BUDGET = 10`, regardless of which warrant is present. There is no per-warrant table any more, and counting does not stop at the decisions heading: content under the heading is charged too, through its own item-shaped allowance rather than being exempt outright. The two are compatible, not contradictory: this prose rule states the *target* shape for the common case (a status turn should need at most 3 lines), while the hook's volume check is the *mechanical backstop* that catches sprawl generally, sized to leave margin above a realistic warranted turn without punishing one for legitimately using more than 3 lines when its warrant calls for it. See §10 for the charge model and free-pool table.

### Answer turns

An Answer turn (Answer warrant PRESENT - see §2 warrant 4) is prose, and the prose IS the payload: no identity line, no status slots, no line cap. This is deliberate, not a gap in the structured shape: "structured outputs are most useful during the actual implementation phase, but during planning not as much," and an operator's direct question deserves a real answer, not a slot template built for status reporting.

If `## Operator decisions` is also present on the same turn (an Answer that also surfaces a decision), it still goes last and still ends the turn - the Answer exemption covers everything ABOVE that heading, never its placement. The heading's own placement-last rule (§4 execution-turn item 3, `content/sections/02-delegation.md`) is universal across both turn shapes.

Answer-turn prose is not exempt from all discipline, only from the structured-shape requirement - see §5 for the relevance rule that governs what the prose may say.

## Length discipline

This section supersedes and absorbs `.agentic/memory/keep-conductor-updates-short.md` into the always-loaded reference tier. §4 removed the line-count framing that used to live here (a hard cap caps the wrong quantity: it truncates a real answer while leaving filler prose in a short one untouched). The rule below replaces it: **on Answer turns, relevance is the discipline, not length.** A LONG answer to a hard question PASSES. A THREE-LINE answer padded with an opening phrase FAILS. That asymmetry is the whole point of dropping the line cap - length was never the actual defect, filler was.

**The relevance rule (Answer turns only).** Every sentence in an Answer turn's prose must be load-bearing for the question asked. Six named bans:

1. Restating the operator's question before answering.
2. Opening preamble ("Good question", "Let me look at that", "Here's what I found").
3. Narrating what the conductor is about to do instead of doing it.
4. Alternatives, trade-offs, or caveats not asked for.
5. Closing recap of what was just said.
6. Self-assessment of the answer's quality or confidence, unless confidence is the question.

Bans 2 and 5 are mechanized advisory-only by `hooks/enforce-turn-shape.py` (`_answer_relevance_flag`, §9) via curated phrase-list regexes. Bans 1, 3, 4, and 6 are **prose-only, deliberately not mechanized**: a real answer necessarily reuses key nouns from the question it answers, so no reliable mechanical heuristic can separate legitimate lexical overlap (ban 1) from genuine restatement, or separate a caveat the operator actually needs from one nobody asked for (ban 4). Ban 3 and ban 6 are similarly semantic judgments about what a sentence is *doing*, not a matchable phrase shape. See §9 for the full non-mechanization rationale.

The six bullets this section previously absorbed from `.agentic/memory/keep-conductor-updates-short.md` each get an explicit disposition against the new warrant-bound §4, rather than being silently dropped:

- *"Default to 1-3 lines per turn. Result first. No preamble, no recap of what I just did."* - **split three ways across both turn shapes, correcting an earlier draft's over-claim that nothing was left to cap.** §4 does NOT remove the line-count framing outright - step 2 explicitly retains "Capped at **1-3 status lines per turn**" verbatim, on the status slots only (`## Operator decisions`, when present, is additional to that cap, not counted against it, per §4). What changed is scope, not existence: the old bullet capped the WHOLE TURN at 1-3 lines; §4's surviving cap covers only the `State`/`Running`/`Blocked` status slots, and is naturally bounded to at most 3 lines by there being exactly three named slots, one line each - there is no fourth slot to add a line through, which is the sense in which the cap is structurally reinforced, not merely restated as a number. On an Answer turn the line count is explicitly uncapped (a long answer can pass); `_volume_flag`'s Answer-turn ceiling (`ANSWER_BODY_BUDGET`, §9) exists only as a runaway-prose backstop, never a shaping cap. "Result first" has no separate rule of its own: it is subsumed by relevance ban 2 (no opening preamble) on Answer turns, and by §4's fixed identity-then-status-then-decisions ORDER on execution turns, where there is no earlier slot to bury a result behind. "No preamble, no recap" survives as relevance bans 2 and 5 above.
- *"Progress pings while agents run: one line, or say nothing."* - **structurally impossible to violate on an execution turn.** A progress ping with no warrant is the silent-continue case (§2, §7 worked non-example): Rule B already forbids writing anything at all, so this bullet is redundant with the emission gate rather than a separate shape rule.
- *"Do NOT explain reasoning, trade-offs, or rejected alternatives unless asked - those belong in the PR body, the plan artifact, or a memory file, not the chat turn."* - **becomes relevance ban 4** on Answer turns (unasked-for alternatives/trade-offs/caveats), and remains structurally impossible on execution turns for the same reason as above. The routing-destination clause (where the excluded content goes) still applies whichever shape the turn takes.
- *"Surface a finding only when it changes what the operator would do. 'Worth knowing about' is usually not."* - **stays verbatim, unchanged.** This is an emission-gate rule (does the finding warrant a turn at all, §2), not a shape rule, and neither turn shape supersedes it.
- *"`## Operator decisions`: the recommended action and one line of why. Nothing else."* - **not restated here, unchanged.** `content/sections/02-delegation.md` and `content/references/delegation-detail.md` §Operator Decisions Block Rationale both already cover this in fuller form (the recommendation-plus-confirmation shape, the "(Recommended)" label convention, the ban on co-equal ballots). A third copy here would be a third place to drift, and this holds regardless of which turn shape the decisions block terminates.
- *"Final completion: what shipped, where it landed, what is left. Not how it was built."* - **stays verbatim, unchanged.** A Completion-warrant execution turn states this in its status slots (no room for "how it was built" prose to leak in); if a Completion warrant co-occurs with an Answer warrant, the same discipline governs the Answer-turn prose instead.

Closing line, verbatim, and now read as governing relevance rather than a raw count: *"Length is the default failure mode here, not brevity. When unsure, cut."* On an Answer turn this means cut what is not load-bearing, not cut until short.

## Worked example - a normal turn

```
DS-123 · fix/foo · [phase: skeptic-review]
State: 2 of 3 units merged, unit 3 in review
Running: skeptic on unit 3 (~4 min)
Blocked: nothing

## Operator decisions
1. <action> (Recommended) - <one line why>. Reply STOP to skip.
```

The `(Recommended)` marker is mandatory even on a single-item list. It is safe to omit today only because the abdication guard's ballot check (`_is_prose_ballot`) requires 2+ items to fire - but the shape shown here is exactly what a second item, added later without noticing the marker was missing, turns into a co-equal ballot that blocks at runtime. Mark the recommended action every time, regardless of item count.

## Worked example - an Answer turn

The operator asks a direct question mid-session. This is the Answer warrant, and the entire turn is the answer - no identity line, no status slots:

```
The Phase 7 quality-gate loop caps at 3 fix-attempt rounds before it surfaces a
BLOCKED to the operator; a 4th failed attempt does not trigger a silent 4th
retry.
```

No identity line, no `State:`/`Running:`/`Blocked:` slots, no line cap - the answer is exactly as long as the question requires and no longer. If the operator's question also produced a genuine decision to surface, `## Operator decisions` would still be appended last, per §4's Answer-turn rule.

## Worked non-example - narrative prose beyond the structured slots

**REJECTED under the new rule (this is the shape DS-156 exists to remove):**

```
DS-140 · fix/bar · [phase: skeptic-review]
State: unit 2 merged, unit 3 skeptic sign-off received

Nice, that closes out the last blocking finding from round 2. I went ahead and
kicked off the PR now that everything is green, and I'll keep an eye on CI -
should only take a few minutes given how small this diff is. Let me know if
you want anything else looked at while that runs.

## Operator decisions
1. Merge PR #612 once CI is green (Recommended) - all checks passing, sign-off
   received. Reply STOP to hold.
```

This is a Completion-warrant (unit merged) turn carrying a narrative paragraph between the status slots and the decisions block - exactly the shape the operator's original complaint named: "all it did was add structured outputs to the top and then just still all the normal prose responses is still there." Under §4, an execution turn's status region may contain only `State:`/`Running:`/`Blocked:`/`Waiting:` lines; the paragraph above is neither, so this turn is a shape violation regardless of which execution warrant fired. `_execution_prose_flag` (§9) is the mechanical check that catches this shape, blocking.

**CORRECT behavior for the same moment:**

```
DS-140 · fix/bar · [phase: skeptic-review]
State: unit 2 merged, unit 3 skeptic sign-off received

## Operator decisions
1. Merge PR #612 once CI is green (Recommended) - all checks passing, sign-off
   received. Reply STOP to hold.
```

Nothing in the middle paragraph was load-bearing for the operator's next action; it was commentary. Drop it.

## Worked example - forced yield

When all work is with background agents and the conductor must end its turn - no decision, no completion, no answer pending, only a stoppage on background work - the turn collapses to a `Waiting:` list:

```
DS-123 · fix/foo · [phase: skeptic-review]
Waiting: skeptic - unit 3 correctness review
Waiting: qa-engineer - unit 3 browser scenarios
```

One `Waiting:` line per agent, naming the agent and what it is waiting for. Nothing else. No state recap, no phase narration, no next-steps, no "meanwhile" commentary, no restating what was already reported in a prior turn. The operator's requirement, as recorded in ticket DS-122: *"It should list the agents it's waiting on and why and that's it. Not a whole bunch of extra stuff, only the important and necessary information always."*

Each `Waiting:` line must be <=120 characters (`WAITING_LINE_MAX_CHARS`) to count as well-formed and free under the volume check (§10). Free-of-charge status is scoped further than that character bound alone: a well-formed `Waiting:` line is free ONLY when `stoppage` is the SOLE warrant present on the turn (DS-151 amendment A1) - the moment any other warrant (decision, completion, answer) co-occurs on the same turn, `Waiting:` lines revert to charging like ordinary lines instead of staying free.

## Worked non-example - the silent continue

**REJECTED (INVALID - no warrant):**

```
DS-123 · fix/foo · [phase: engineer-spawned]
State: engineer returned unit 2, spawning skeptic now
```

This carries no decision, no `Waiting:` line, no completion phrase, and answers no operator question. It is a status ping, and per Rule B it should not have been written at all.

**CORRECT behavior for the same moment:** say nothing. Spawn the skeptic and keep working. The next turn written is whichever of the four warrants fires next - most likely the skeptic's findings surfacing a decision, or a forced-yield `Waiting:` turn if the conductor's own work is now exhausted and only background agents remain.

## Edge case: interaction with the abdication guard

This section documents an **interaction**, not a "no conflict" - the honest claim is that the two hooks never contradict, not that they are unrelated. `hooks/enforce-no-abdication.py` runs three classifiers OR-gated together: `_is_abdication` (the classic permission-seeking interrogative), `_is_stalled_surface_and_proceed` (an announced-then-unexecuted default), and `_is_prose_ballot` (a co-equal ballot lacking a `(Recommended)`/`Recommendation:` marker). The turn-shape hook described in §10 below is a separate mechanism. Three points establish how they relate:

1. **Severity is no longer uniform across the turn-shape hook itself (DS-156).** `_execution_prose_flag` (the execution-turn structural shape check, §9) is BLOCKING: it can exit non-zero and block the current stop, the same category of outcome the abdication guard produces. `_answer_relevance_flag` (the Answer-turn ban 2/5 check, §9) remains advisory-only: it always exits 0, its finding surfaces as additional context on the *next* turn, and it never blocks. So this is no longer "only one of the two hooks can ever block a turn" - it is "one specific check inside the turn-shape hook now can, and the abdication guard always could." See §9 for why the split lands where it does.
2. **The axes are disjoint.** The turn-shape hook inspects structural shape and *warrant presence* - whether a `## Operator decisions` heading exists at all, a boolean. It never inspects whether individual decision items carry a `(Recommended)`/`Recommendation:` marker; that inspection belongs exclusively to `_is_prose_ballot`. Because the two hooks check different properties of the text, they cannot contradict each other - at most both fire additively on the same turn (an "operator-decisions item sprawl" finding from one, a "fix your ballot markers" finding from the other, both legitimately triggerable by the same sprawling, unmarked decisions block), and those two findings are never mutually exclusive.
3. **Convergent steady state.** A spec-compliant turn - correct fixed-slot shape, and any `## Operator decisions` items carrying a `(Recommended)` marker - suppresses `_is_abdication` (a documented negative-gate coupling: the marker is one of the tokens `_is_abdication` inspects) and suppresses `_is_prose_ballot` (no unmarked items to flag). The turn-shape hook's own forced-yield shape check is separately gated off in this case because the `decision` warrant is present. All three mechanisms independently agree there is nothing to flag on a correctly-shaped turn - arrived at by two unrelated code paths, not by one hook deferring to the other.

State plainly: **a silent continue is the opposite of abdication.** The abdication guard fires on permission-seeking language and on an announced-then-unexecuted default. It never fires on silently continuing work with no turn written at all - that is exactly the behavior Rule B (§2) mandates for the non-warranted case, and the two mechanisms agree on it.

## Hook contract

`hooks/enforce-turn-shape.py` mechanically implements the warrant/shape/relevance/volume checks in §2, §4, §5, and §7. It does NOT implement the execution-turn identity-line convention (§4) - that was removed, not merely left unimplemented; see the history note at the end of this section. This subsection describes the CONTRACT the hook satisfies as of DS-156 Unit 2.

**Two checks, two enforcement postures (DS-156, operator decision - overrides the architect's blanket-advisory recommendation).** Where the prior version of this hook ran a single uniformly-advisory check, it now runs two checks with different postures:

- **`_execution_prose_flag` - BLOCKING.** Implements §4's execution-turn structural rule. **This REPLACES `_forced_yield_flag`; it does not run alongside it.** Its domain is NOT a single flat set - it depends on whether `stoppage` is the SOLE warrant present on the turn. **On BOTH branches, the identity line at position 1 is checked for length only (never shape) against the single `STATUS_LINE_MAX_CHARS` definition in §4** - the bound is a property of position 1, not of which branch is running, so this is stated once here rather than restated per branch below:
  - **Sole-stoppage branch.** Inspects every non-blank RAW line after the identity line, fenced or not (`_body_after_identity_line`'s domain - the same one `_forced_yield_flag` inspects today). Only a line matching the `Waiting:` shape (§4) is permitted there; any other line, fenced or unfenced, is flagged. The identity line sits before this raw-line domain and is covered by the shared position-1 length check above, not by this bullet.
  - **General branch (decision and/or completion present, with or without stoppage).** Inspects only the unfenced lines of the fence-aware status region (`_segment`/`_regions`). `State:`/`Running:`/`Blocked:` slot lines and `Waiting:`-shaped lines are permitted in the status region; anything else unfenced is flagged, and a permitted slot line exceeding `STATUS_LINE_MAX_CHARS` is flagged the same way regardless of otherwise matching the whitelist shape - the identity line's own length is covered by the shared position-1 check above, not restated here. `Waiting:` lines are exempt from this length bound by design (see §4). Fenced content in the status region is outside the check's domain - see the Known uncovered shapes table below.
  On a finding, it exits non-zero and blocks the current stop, injecting a directive to reshape the turn - the same class of outcome the abdication guard already produces, not a new mechanism. It additionally catches the structural violation the worked non-example above shows (a Completion-warrant turn with narrative prose in its unfenced status region), which `_forced_yield_flag` never inspected because that check only ever ran on sole-stoppage turns.
- **`_answer_relevance_flag` - ADVISORY.** Implements §5's relevance bans 2 (opening preamble) and 5 (closing recap) against Answer-turn prose via curated phrase-list regexes. On a finding, it emits `{"hookSpecificOutput": {"hookEventName": "Stop", "additionalContext": "TURN-SHAPE: <finding>"}}` and exits 0 - unconditionally, this specific check can never block a stop. This is the SAME posture the whole hook carried before DS-156; nothing about ban 2/5 enforcement changes except that it now applies only when the Answer warrant fired (an execution turn has no prose region for these bans to inspect).

**Why the split, not one posture for both.** `_execution_prose_flag` is a structural predicate with no phrase matching or inference involved - a false positive requires the conductor to have actually violated the shape. Advisory enforcement of exactly this structural problem has now shipped three times (DS-122, DS-151, DS-155) and the prose never went away; that is a measured 3-for-3 failure rate for advisory enforcement here, and `docs/overview/vision.md` pillar 2 (verifiable outcomes) treats a hook that logs and exits 0 as having produced no verifiable outcome. `_answer_relevance_flag` stays advisory because its two mechanized bans are curated phrase lists, not semantic detectors - they mis-fire on novel phrasing, and blocking a genuine answer over an opening phrase like "Good question" is a real friction cost against the same vision pillar's concern for bounded, load-bearing operator interruption. The asymmetry is deliberate, not an oversight: a structural yes/no check earns blocking; a heuristic phrase match does not.

**Non-mechanization of bans 1, 3, 4, 6 (§5), with reasoning.** Only bans 2 and 5 are mechanized. The other four relevance bans - restating the question (1), narrating instead of doing (3), unasked-for alternatives/trade-offs/caveats (4), and self-assessment of answer quality (6) - are prose-only rules, deliberately not implemented as regex checks. A real answer necessarily reuses key nouns from the question it answers, so no reliable mechanical heuristic separates legitimate lexical overlap from genuine restatement (ban 1); "narrating instead of doing" and "self-assessing" are judgments about what a sentence is DOING, not a matchable shape (bans 3, 6); and "not asked for" (ban 4) requires knowing what WAS asked, which a phrase-list regex has no access to. These four remain human/model judgment calls, enforced only by this file's prose and by review, never by the hook.

- **Config toggle.** `turn_shape_guard_enabled` in `.agentic/config.json`, **default ON when the key is absent**, governing BOTH checks together (there is no separate toggle per check). This default is deliberately inverted from `abdication_guard_enabled` (which defaults OFF when absent and requires explicit opt-in): now that `_execution_prose_flag` can block, the risk profile is closer to symmetric with the abdication guard than it was pre-DS-156, but the operator decision retains the existing default-on posture rather than introducing a second opt-in gate for the same hook.
- **Kill switch.** `AE_TURN_SHAPE_GUARD_DISABLE=1` disables per-session, matching the convention used by the abdication guard's own kill switch (`hooks/enforce-no-abdication.py`). Disables both checks; there is no per-check kill switch.
- **Loop guard.** Both checks share the existing loop-guard machinery via `hooks/lib/loop_guard.py` (the same two-layer design used by `enforce-no-abdication.py`: the `stop_hook_active` payload flag, and a per-`cwd` counter cap as a backstop for CC bug #54360). For `_answer_relevance_flag` this bounds advisory re-invocation exactly as before DS-156. For `_execution_prose_flag`, now that a finding blocks rather than merely re-invokes with additional context, ONE shared counter/cap (`CONSECUTIVE_BLOCK_CAP = 2`) governs BOTH checks together - a block and an advisory are both "this hook re-invoked the model" events from the loop guard's perspective, so it must not allow an unbounded block loop on a conductor that keeps writing the same non-conforming shape.
  - **Bounding re-invocation count does not bound visible duplicate output (DS-155), and this residual is UNCHANGED for `_answer_relevance_flag`.** The Claude Code harness does not retract or replace the already-streamed flagged turn when `additionalContext` re-invokes the model - the operator sees BOTH the original flagged message AND the corrected re-invocation, back to back, for every single advisory fire. This residual applies only to the advisory check; a blocking check does not stream the rejected turn's content to the operator in the same way. See residual false positive 4 below.
- **Classification order.** Warrant classification (§2) runs first and is authoritative, and determines which check even applies - but the applicable partition is THREE-WAY, not the Answer/execution two-way split alone: (1) **Answer turns** (Answer warrant present) route to `_answer_relevance_flag` only; (2) **execution turns** (Answer warrant absent, at least one of Decision/Stoppage/Completion present, per §4) route to `_execution_prose_flag`; (3) **zero-warrant turns** (Answer warrant absent, and none of Decision/Stoppage/Completion present either) route to neither - that case belongs exclusively to `_status_only_flag`, which stays at its existing ADVISORY posture, unchanged by DS-156. A turn cannot trigger both `_execution_prose_flag` and `_answer_relevance_flag` findings, because §4's Answer/execution split is exhaustive and mutually exclusive OVER THE SET OF WARRANTED TURNS - but that split does not itself cover the unwarranted case, and gating `_execution_prose_flag` on "Answer warrant absent" alone (rather than "Answer warrant absent AND at least one other warrant present") would incorrectly pull the zero-warrant case into blocking enforcement nobody authorized. Implement the three-way gate explicitly; do not collapse it to a two-way "Answer present/absent" test.
- **Volume check (DS-151), with a raised Answer ceiling, not a short-circuit (DS-156).** A mechanical backstop for the length-discipline promise above: it computes a single whole-message CHARGE. On an execution turn it compares that charge against `BASE_BODY_BUDGET = 10`, unchanged from DS-151 - there is no per-warrant table for execution turns. **On an Answer turn, `_volume_flag` does NOT short-circuit to no-charge.** It instead compares the same charge against a separate, deliberately high, ADVISORY-only ceiling: `ANSWER_BODY_BUDGET = 50` (5x `BASE_BODY_BUDGET`) - this is a runaway-prose backstop, not a shaping constraint. §5 explicitly uncaps Answer-turn length - a long answer to a hard question passes by design, and a real answer to a hard question should sit comfortably under 50 non-blank lines - so `ANSWER_BODY_BUDGET` exists to catch a degenerate case (a runaway generation loop producing far more prose than any legitimate single-turn answer needs), never to bound a legitimate long answer. 50 is a deliberately loose multiple of `BASE_BODY_BUDGET` chosen to sit well above a realistic detailed answer while still catching sustained runaway output; it is not sized against a "thousands of lines" failure mode specifically - see the Known uncovered shapes table below for the resulting gap. Because it is advisory, tripping it never blocks the turn; it only surfaces a finding on the next turn, consistent with `_answer_relevance_flag`'s posture on the same turn shape. On an execution turn, the charge mechanism is unchanged from DS-151: every non-blank line in the status region charges 1 unless it is a well-formed `Waiting:` line **and** `stoppage` is the SOLE warrant present, in which case it charges 0; fenced content in the status region is scored as a single aggregate pool (the first `FENCE_FREE_LINES` fenced lines are free, everything past that charges full weight, regardless of how many separate fences it is split across). Every non-blank line under the `## Operator decisions` heading charges 1 unless it falls inside a recognized item's first `ITEM_FREE_LINES` lines - fenced content inside an item counts toward that item's own line total, it does not escape into the status-region fence pool, so it is charged exactly once. The check is skipped entirely only when zero warrants are present (that case belongs exclusively to `_status_only_flag` above); it is never skipped on an Answer turn, only re-budgeted against `ANSWER_BODY_BUDGET` and demoted to advisory. See the table immediately below for the full free-pool definitions (the free pools apply on execution turns; an Answer turn's charge is a flat non-blank-line count against `ANSWER_BODY_BUDGET`, since none of the execution-turn free-pool machinery - status slots, `Waiting:` lines, decision items - constrains Answer-turn shape).

  | Free pool | Cap | Scope |
  |---|---|---|
  | `FENCE_FREE_LINES` | 20 lines | Aggregate across every fence in the status region (not per fence) |
  | Well-formed `Waiting:` lines | unbounded line count, each <=`WAITING_LINE_MAX_CHARS` (120) chars | Per line, ONLY when `stoppage` is the sole warrant present on the turn |
  | `ITEM_FREE_LINES` | 3 lines | Per recognized `## Operator decisions` item (fenced or not), unbounded item count |
  | The `## Operator decisions` heading line itself | always free | n/a - counted in neither region |

  Two things worth stating explicitly about how these pools behave, since neither is obvious from the table alone:
  - **Sole-stoppage forced-yield turns are not skipped outright any more; they charge 0 by construction.** The deleted exclusion model exempted a sole-stoppage turn from measurement entirely. Under the charge model there is nothing to exempt: every well-formed `Waiting:` line charges 0 while `stoppage` is the SOLE warrant present (amendment A1), so a clean forced-yield turn's charge is 0 regardless of how many `Waiting:` lines it carries - the same "unbounded per-instance" property as before, just enforced structurally rather than by a skip branch. The moment ANY other warrant (decision, completion, answer) co-occurs, every `Waiting:` line reverts to charging 1 like ordinary prose - a well-formed-but-non-sole-warrant `Waiting:` line is no longer free.
  - **The free pools are NOT uniformly "aggregate per message"** (amendment A5): the fence pool is aggregate across the status region; the `ITEM_FREE_LINES` allowance is per item, with an unbounded item count; the well-formed-`Waiting:` allowance is per line, also with an unbounded line count when stoppage is the sole warrant. Only the fence pool pools multiple instances into one scalar - the other two stay individually capped per instance while allowing unbounded instance count.

  What counts toward the charge: fenced content in the STATUS region participates in the single aggregate pool above - the first `FENCE_FREE_LINES` fenced lines across every fence in the status region are free, and every fenced line past that charges full weight, so splitting one long fence into many short ones gains nothing (the pool is aggregate, not per fence). An UNCLOSED fence (the message ends mid-fence, or any other odd count of `` ``` `` delimiters) gets NO exclusion at all - `_segment` fails closed and treats an unmatched trailing opener as never validly fenced, so every line collected since it charges full weight as ordinary prose. Fenced content INSIDE a recognized `## Operator decisions` item folds into that item's own `ITEM_FREE_LINES` allowance instead of the status-region fence pool - it is charged once, through the item, never twice (double-charging it in both pools would risk pushing the charge above the message's own non-blank line count). The `## Operator decisions` block's total line count IS now part of the whole-message charge, unlike the deleted exclusion model where it was never bounded by this check at all: every line under the heading that does not fall inside a recognized item's first `ITEM_FREE_LINES` lines charges 1, so an unrecognized or sprawling decisions region can push the message over budget on its own (see the next bullet for the separate PER-ITEM shape bound, which is additional to this whole-message charge, not a substitute for it).
- **No character bound on ordinary prose lines IN THE VOLUME CHECK.** This bullet describes `_volume_flag`'s charge model only, not the separate structural shape check above. A verbose turn written as long paragraphs still charges 1 per non-blank line in the volume check regardless of that line's length; only `Waiting:` lines carry a character bound (`WAITING_LINE_MAX_CHARS`) there, because only `Waiting:` lines can ever be unboundedly free in that check. `STATUS_LINE_MAX_CHARS` (§4, above) is a DIFFERENT bound on a DIFFERENT check - it lives in the BLOCKING shape check (`_execution_prose_flag`), not the advisory volume check, and governs slot/identity lines rather than `Waiting:` lines. The two bounds do not overlap in what they govern and neither substitutes for the other.

- **Operator-decisions item-sprawl check (DS-151).** A second, independent mechanical check: item COUNT under `## Operator decisions` stays completely unbounded, per `content/sections/02-delegation.md`'s ban on a numeric item cap - but each individual item's LINE COUNT is bounded at `ITEM_FREE_LINES` (3) lines (aliased `MAX_LINES_PER_DECISION_ITEM`), matching the mandated "recommended action, one line of why, and the reversal offer" shape quoted above. An item (a numbered or bulleted top-level line, with any continuation lines - fenced or not - folded into it) that sprawls past 3 lines is flagged by name. This is a different axis than the banned item-count cap - ten one-line items pass; one forty-line item does not.
- **Registration is guarded**, unlike its sibling hooks: `test -f <script> && python3 <script> || exit 0`. This is deliberate - a missing script under the unguarded pattern used by other hooks would exit 2 (the blocking code) as an UNINTENDED side effect of a revert or partial checkout, which is a distinct failure from `_execution_prose_flag`'s own INTENDED blocking exit on a genuine finding. The guard exists to prevent the accidental case, not to preserve an advisory-only contract the hook no longer uniformly has.
- **Fail-open.** A top-level exception guard wraps the hook body; any unexpected error exits 0 rather than surfacing as a block. This applies identically to both checks - an unexpected exception must never manufacture a spurious block from `_execution_prose_flag` any more than a spurious advisory from `_answer_relevance_flag`.
- **Enforcement log.** Participates in `hooks/lib/enforcement_log.py` via `log_fire(...)`, called only on the finding branch (no log entry on a clean turn). `_answer_relevance_flag` logs `"allow_advisory"`, matching the hook's pre-DS-156 behavior. `_execution_prose_flag` logs `"deny"` - the same decision string `enforce-tier.py`, `enforce-shippable-edit.py`, and the other genuinely-blocking hooks in this repo use - so the enforcement log's decision field distinguishes the two checks' severity without needing a third value.

**Four documented residual false positives / costs** - accepted trade-offs, not defects to fix:

1. A stoppage-only turn that adds a separate explanatory sentence beside the `Waiting:` line is flagged, because the hook expects the `Waiting:` line to be self-contained. Fold the reason into the line itself instead of appending prose: `Waiting: engineer - unit 3, blocks merge` rather than a `Waiting:` line followed by a sentence explaining why.
2. A `Waiting:` turn that also answers the operator's preceding question can be misclassified. The `answer` warrant detector (§2, warrant 4) is deliberately weak - best-effort quoted-fragment matching, plus a transcript-derived question bonus (see below) - and can still miss a genuine answer folded into a `Waiting:` turn, causing the turn to collapse to the sole-stoppage forced-yield shape and get flagged even though it also carried a real answer.
3. (DS-151) A legitimate paste over `FENCE_FREE_LINES` (20) aggregate lines in the status region charges its excess even when it is a single honest fence, not sprawling prose - a 40-line diff pasted into the status region charges 20 and fires. The pool is aggregate across the whole status region, not per fence, so splitting the paste into several shorter fences gains nothing; there is no mechanical way to distinguish a legitimately long paste from an abusive one without the pool losing its purpose. Spec-aligned, not a defect: this content belongs in the PR body, the plan artifact, or a memory file instead (§5, "routes... not the chat turn").
4. (DS-155) **Every advisory fire, by design, produces visible duplicate operator-facing output** - not a false positive in the classification sense, but a UX cost inherent to the `additionalContext` re-invocation mechanism (see the Loop guard bullet above). The harness streams both the original flagged turn and the corrected re-invocation to the operator; the loop guard bounds how many TIMES this can happen (`CONSECUTIVE_BLOCK_CAP = 2`), not whether the operator sees the duplication when it does happen. Accepted because the alternative - suppressing the original streamed turn - is not something a Stop hook can do on this harness; named here so a future design does not assume the loop-count bound also solved the duplicate-output problem, which it does not.

**DS-155 answer-bonus narrowing (not fully closed - a named residual gap remains, disclosed below):** a genuine plain-prose answer to a direct operator question no longer requires the narrow quoted-fragment `answer` shape (`_transcript_answer_bonus`), gated on the most recent GENUINE transcript message looking like a question AND remaining the immediately preceding turn boundary - no genuinely separate, earlier completed assistant turn since it was asked (`_has_intervening_assistant_turn`). This recency check has been rewritten three times against demonstrated false positives, each measured against a local Claude Code transcript corpus (900 files / ~1,623 completed-turn evaluation points, unless noted otherwise) rather than assumed from a hand-built fixture:

- Round 3 tried to exempt the model's own earlier "narrate, then call a tool" step from counting as a competing intervening turn, keyed on an assistant entry whose content array mixed a `text` block and a `tool_use` block in the SAME entry. A corpus measurement (3,429 files, 169,745 assistant entries) found that shape occurs 4 times (0.002%) - round 3's fix protected against a shape that essentially does not occur, so the false positive it was meant to close remained open in practice. Correctness: 1,025 / 1,623 (63.2%).
- Round 4 rewrote the check against the MEASURED shape instead: Claude Code writes `text` and `tool_use` as SEPARATE entries (the same corpus measured this split-entry pattern - a pure-text entry immediately followed by a separate `tool_use` entry - 30,027 times). The fix tracked tool_use presence ACROSS entries during the reverse scan, consumed by at most one preceding text entry. Correctness rose to 1,580 / 1,623 (97.4%), but pure POSITION cannot distinguish "this text precedes ITS OWN later tool call" from "this text is a genuinely separate, already-completed turn that happens to be followed by an UNRELATED turn's tool call" - both look identical by position alone. Demonstrated live: a completed turn immediately followed by new work opening with a tool call was silently excused as belonging to that new work.
- Round 5 (current) scopes the exemption to a SHARED `message.id` instead of position - every real transcript entry carries this field, and entries belonging to one logical assistant message share it; that is the actual delimiter position was only ever a proxy for. Corpus-verified: of 5,480 real adjacent (pure-text, tool_use) entry pairs, 5,479 (99.98%) share one message.id. Correctness: 1,620 / 1,623 (99.8%).

All three rounds also carry a fix for a separate false negative: when the current turn's own entry had not yet been written to the transcript at Stop time, the genuinely earlier turn already on disk was being silently treated as "this turn's own", so a stale-by-one question incorrectly still granted the bonus. A completed-turn boundary marker is confirmed by an EXACT TEXT MATCH against the turn under evaluation, not by position. `hooks/tests/fixtures/turn-shape-corpus-samples.json` (structural entry shapes and message.id-sharing PATTERN only - opaque relabeled placeholders, no real transcript content) and a corpus-replay regression test, stratified by shape class and asserting BOTH the QUIET and the ADVISORY direction, pin this fix directly against measured real-world shapes rather than a hand-built fixture - a one-sided, position-stratified version of this same gate is precisely what let the round-4 defect ship unnoticed for one round.

**Named residual, round 5 (3 of 1,623, not rounded to zero):** all 3 remaining wrong evaluation points share one shape - a pure-text entry and its own later tool_use entry share message.id, but a harness-injected/system bookkeeping line is interleaved between them in the transcript. The id-set match does not require the two entries to be contiguous, so it still treats them as one message (correctly, in the sense that they are one API response) and exempts the text entry; a stricter definition requiring unbroken transcript-line contiguity to group same-id entries would instead flag it. A contiguity-tracking refinement is a possible further fix, not implemented this round.

**DS-156: the completion warrant was widened against a measured corpus, the same discipline as the DS-155 answer-bonus work above.** The reported symptom: a turn opening with a bolded `Done.` and reporting a genuine finished multi-step operation was flagged status-only, because the pre-DS-156 `completion` warrant matched only `[phase: complete]` or a small hardcoded phrase list. Method (`~/.claude/projects` - a live, growing directory; the extraction script is not committed, so this is a point-in-time snapshot rather than a reproducible figure - measured at 3,341 transcript files, 165,965 assistant entries, 6,760 candidate completed final-turn texts grouped by `message.id`, i.e. the 3,535 main-agent + 3,225 sidechain split cited in round 3 below):

- Round 1 tried 7 candidate phrases matched ANYWHERE in the turn, including a `merged/shipped and (live|deployed)` phrase, measured against the UNFILTERED corpus (1,231 turns already flagged status-only). A 50-item hand-verified sample of newly-matched turns found 3 false positives, all sharing one shape: the matching phrase described a COMPLETED SUB-ITEM inside a turn whose OVERALL state was still in-progress (CI running elsewhere, another unit still building, or a future-tense "I'll report when it's merged and live") - and in all 3, the matching text was not on the identity line.
- Round 2 (shipped as commit `cf6bc9d5`) moved the past-participle phrase, plus a new leading "X is done/complete" sentence pattern, to match the identity line ONLY (a string-start anchor, never scanned line-by-line through the body). An 80-item hand-labelled precision sample of newly-recognised completions found 0 false positives; a parallel 80-item recall sample of turns still flagged after the fix found the residual gap is real but small - most commonly a completion declared under a markdown sub-heading rather than the identity line. Reported net effect on the full 1,231-turn UNFILTERED flagged set: 198 (16.1%) newly recognised as completion.
- **Round 3 (this correction) found round 2's entire measurement was drawn from the wrong population.** `hooks/enforce-turn-shape.py` is registered on the `Stop` event only, which fires for the main agent; subagent turns fire the unregistered `SubagentStop` event instead - so the "18.3%-flagged, 1,231-turn" corpus round 1 and round 2 both measured against was ~48% turns this hook never evaluates (splitting by `isSidechain`: 3,535 main-agent / 3,225 sidechain candidate final turns). Re-derived main-agent-only: pre-fix flagged status-only was 981 (not 1,231); round 2's fix recognised 103 of those (10.5%, not 198/16.1%). Independently hand-labelling all 103 (not a sample) found 4 genuine false positives and 7 borderline turns, all invisible in round 2's own 80-item sample because that sample's pool was itself majority sidechain `Status: DONE` engineer returns, which diluted the real conductor-population false positives to invisibility. Two of round 2's structured-field sentinels, `status: DONE` / `status: DONE_WITH_CONCERNS` and `review complete`, turned out to have **zero** main-agent contribution - all their hits were sidechain - and `status: DONE` specifically is actively harmful on a conductor turn, since its only real appearance there is a RELAY of a subagent's own return text, not a self-report. Both are deleted outright. Round 3 also dropped the comma from the leading-declaration terminator class (a trailing comma matched a leading CLAUSE, not a sentence, silently ignoring whatever continuation followed it) and added a continuing-work veto - a non-empty Conductor-template `Running:` field, or one of three phrases ("one to go", "remaining after", "is/are still running") derived from the 4 confirmed real false positives - that suppresses the completion warrant outright when present anywhere in the turn. Net effect on the 981-turn main-agent population: 90 (9.2%) newly recognised as completion, 891 (90.8%) remain correctly flagged status-only. Independently re-labelling all 90 (not a sample) found 0 confirmed false positives, and one disclosed, accepted trade-off: a genuine completion whose closing aside mentions an unrelated OTHER session "still running" is over-suppressed by the same phrase that correctly catches the real false positives - not chased further, since distinguishing "my own dependent work" from "an unrelated process" needs semantic understanding a lexical guard does not have.
