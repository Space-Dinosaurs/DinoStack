Outcome rubrics and qa_criteria: defining done before the work starts

2026-11-06

DinoStack writes down what "done" means before an engineer starts, in fields a reviewer can check the work against. For Elevated work, three of them carry the weight: Verification, the outcome rubric, and qa_criteria.

Where done gets written

Planning artifacts are triggered mechanically, after the orchestration planner returns a unit count. Trivial and Low work needs none. Elevated work with 0 or 1 Elevated units uses the architect plan alone. 2 to 5 Elevated units need a Brief on top of the architect plan, and 6 or more, work spanning two or more tracks, or work spanning two or more sessions needs a full Plan. A task can be promoted to a heavier artifact mid-work, never demoted.

The Brief is meant to fit on one screen: the problem, up to four observable success criteria, non-goals, hard constraints, then the three fields this post is about. The architect plan has its own obligation: a qa_criteria block is mandatory on every Elevated plan, whether or not a Brief exists.

Verification is not the rubric

Verification is a single line that cannot be skipped. It names the tests, gates, qa.md trigger patterns, and any regression tests the findings record requires. If verification cannot be specified at planning time, that is itself a planning gap, and the Brief is not eligible for Skeptic review until it is named.

The outcome rubric is different: it is your semantic definition of done, as at most 6 terse pass/fail lines that you confirm. Each line is tagged with a verification_type. A deterministic line names a gate that proves it, such as tests, lint, a schema check or an HTTP status. A judgment line is qualitative, such as "the new flow is coherent from an operator perspective", and is graded by the independent Skeptic. The rubric can be drafted by the product-discovery agent or by /ds-brief, which can also pull candidates out of a PRD's "Definition of Done" or "Acceptance Criteria" headings, capped at 6. Either way it is confirmed before the Brief is written. It is required for Elevated work and not for Trivial or Low.

How the Skeptic grades a rubric

  • A rubric that is absent or empty on Elevated work is a Critical finding.
  • A line with no verification_type is a Major finding.
  • For a deterministic line, the Skeptic checks that the named gate was actually run and passed. A gate that was not run is Major.
  • A judgment line is graded adversarially and independently. The Skeptic must not treat it as self-certifying, and an unmet line is Major.

Majors block sign-off unless the Worker documents a compelling reason to defer, so a rubric line nobody met cannot ship quietly.

qa_criteria: the test plan, written before the code

qa_criteria is a YAML block that decides whether the qa-engineer runs, and that the qa-engineer treats as its authoritative test plan. It has two shapes:

  • A skip, where qa_skip is one of five values (pure-backend-library, config-only, type-only-refactor, dep-bump-no-runtime-change, docs-only) and qa_skip_rationale says why, in 200 characters or fewer. The architect may not write "n/a" instead.
  • Scenarios, each with an id, one observable sentence describing it, a method, and the evidence expected. The method is one of seven: browser, api, runtime-required, visual_conformance, accessibility, perceptual_diff and motion. A viewport list picks from mobile (375x667), tablet (768x1024) and desktop (1440x900), defaulting to desktop only.

The architect has rules for filling it in. Every UI-visible Elevated unit that runs QA needs an accessibility scenario, at WCAG AA by default. A UI-visible unit whose ticket has an Expected Result or visual spec needs a visual_conformance scenario. A clearly responsive change needs at least [mobile, desktop]. A missing qa_criteria block on Elevated work is a Critical finding, and an invalid qa_skip value is a Major one.

When QA actually runs

QA fires for every full-route Elevated unit unless qa_skip is one of the five values; a project having no qa.md is not a reason to skip it. When it fires, the qa-engineer runs in parallel with the Skeptic and sign-off requires both to pass. On the lighter single-review route, QA fires only for a user-visible UI change, with the acceptance text as its criteria. One practical caveat: motion scenarios need Playwright, because agent-browser cannot execute them.

Plans close the loop

A full Plan adds a verification gate with a "Rubric lines resolved" section: each deterministic line with its gate command and result, each judgment line with its grader, the Skeptic, and result. The gate cannot be skipped, and any "cannot specify" entry blocks Skeptic sign-off until the operator re-runs the architect, tightens the Brief, or descopes.

Why it matters

Writing done down first, as gate commands, operator-confirmed rubric lines and a runtime test plan, gives the reviewer and the QA agent something fixed to check against, and makes "done" mean the same thing before and after the work.
