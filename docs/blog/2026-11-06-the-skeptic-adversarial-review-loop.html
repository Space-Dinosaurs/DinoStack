The Skeptic: DinoStack's adversarial review loop

2026-11-06

A model reviewing its own work tends to agree with itself. DinoStack's answer is the Skeptic: for every change classified as Elevated risk, a separate reviewer agent critiques the work before it ships. There is no self-review path for Elevated work.

A fresh reviewer with no shared context

The core idea is that a reviewer's value is its independence. A reviewer that has already heard the implementer's justifications is partly anchored to them. So each Skeptic is a fresh agent, never a continuation of an earlier round. It receives three things: the adversarial brief describing what to probe, the Worker's output, and a list of issues already resolved in earlier rounds. It never sees the Worker's reasoning. The Skeptic is also read-only: it can read, search, and run commands, but it cannot edit files or spawn other agents.

Critical, Major, Minor

The Skeptic classifies every finding, and an unclassified finding counts as Major.

  • Critical blocks sign-off: security vulnerabilities, correctness failures, data-loss paths. A Critical earns a round of its own.
  • Major blocks sign-off unless the Worker gives a compelling, documented reason to defer it: missing error handling on a critical path, an edge case that fails silently. Open Majors are batched into one fix round rather than one round each.
  • Minor does not block sign-off by default. A fix agent applies Minors after sign-off, with no follow-up review.

Sign-off is the plain statement that no Critical or Major finding is left unresolved.

A round cap, and an operator grant to go past it

Review can go on forever if nothing stops it. By default a unit gets 3 Skeptic rounds (2 on the lighter single-review route). At the cap the conductor does exactly one of two things: it ships, recording every unresolved non-Critical finding in the PR body as accepted debt, or it escalates to you with the cost so far. An unresolved Critical always blocks; the cap never ships one.

The cap is enforced by a hook, not by memory. hooks/enforce-skeptic-round-cap.py counts rounds per unit and denies the next Skeptic spawn once the cap is reached. Only an operator grant reopens it: a reply such as "grant", or the skeptic-grant- token the denial prints for that unit and round. Nothing the conductor writes to its own state file can lift the cap.

Briefs may not prime the reviewer

Independence is easy to undo by accident. If the conductor writes "look hard at the retry logic, I think the backoff resets on the wrong condition" into the Skeptic's brief, the reviewer starts from the conductor's conclusion. DinoStack bans that shape. A spawn brief may not carry the conductor's own hypothesis, suspicion, or attention-steer, whether stated as a fact, a suspicion, or a leading question. Two things that look similar are allowed, because they can be checked against something other than the conductor's say-so: a factual claim tagged with where it was verified, and content passed through with explicit attribution to a named agent, such as an engineer's own stated concerns. Every factual claim the conductor writes into a spawn brief carries a provenance tag, so the Skeptic can tell a verified fact from an unverified one.

Why it matters

The loop is built around the failure it is meant to catch: an implementer, human or model, quietly ratifying its own mistakes. A fresh reviewer, a severity scale tied to sign-off, a hard round cap, and briefs that cannot carry the conductor's conclusions together make the review independent in practice, not just in name.
